Mastery by section

FINAL MASTERY REVIEW — high-yield evening-before revision sheet

Plural decision tree

1. Is the plural irregular or unchanged? Use the learned form: child → children; sheep → sheep. 2. Consonant + y? Change y to ies. Vowel + y? Add s. 3. Ends in s, ss, sh, ch, x, or many z words? Add es. 4. Some f/fe words change to ves—but check lexical exceptions such as roofs and chiefs. 5. Some consonant + o words take es (heroes); important exceptions take s (photos, pianos). 6. Otherwise, add s.

Countability decision tree

Ask what the noun means here—not just how it is spelled. Separate units? → countable → a/an, numbers, plural forms. Mass, substance, abstraction, or undivided amount? → uncountable → no ordinary a/an or plural s. Need to count a mass noun? Count a unit: three pieces of information; two loaves of bread. Meaning can switch: chicken (animal/meat), paper (article/material), room (area/space).

Quantifier selection

Plural countable → many, few/a few, several, fewer, number of. Uncountable → much, little/a little, less, amount of. Both → some, any, a lot of, enough, more, most. Positive small quantity: a few / a little. Scarcity: few / little. Some in offers and expected-positive requests; any in negatives, neutral questions, and free-choice meanings.

Personal-pronoun decision tree

1. Find the antecedent. 2. Determine person: speaker, listener, or other. 3. Determine number: singular or plural. 4. Determine grammatical role—not merely word position. Subject/doer → I, we, he, she, it, they. Object/after preposition → me, us, him, her, it, them. Remove the other name in coordination: “praised Sam and me” → “praised me.”

Reflexive-pronoun tree

Does the pronoun refer back to the clause subject? Match person and number: I → myself; we → ourselves; they → themselves. Is it required as the participant affected by the action? → reflexive. Can you delete it while keeping the core event complete? → probably emphatic/intensive. Never use nonstandard hisself/theirselves or use myself merely to sound formal.

Adjective or determiner?

Does the word describe or classify the noun? → adjective: beautiful, old, medical. Does it identify, possess, select, or quantify the noun? → determiner/quantifier: my, this, every, three, many. School labels may call my a possessive adjective, this a demonstrative adjective, and three an adjective of number. Give the school answer first; understand the modern structure too.

Top exam traps

  • Do not pluralise ordinary uncountables: information, advice, furniture, luggage, homework.
  • Choose quantifiers by countability before thinking about size: many books, much information.
  • Few/little are negative in meaning; a few/a little are small but positive.
  • Use object forms after verbs and prepositions: called him; between you and me.
  • A reflexive must have an appropriate antecedent: “I completed it,” not “Myself completed it.”
  • A possessive determiner needs a noun (their house); a possessive pronoun stands alone (theirs).
  • Function controls labels: this is a pronoun in “This is mine,” but a determiner in “this book.”
  • Adjectives can be predicative or postpositive: “seems tired”; “something useful.”
  • Naturalness is not the same as grammar: “much money” is grammatical but often formal or less conversational in affirmative speech.
  • Learn accepted variants and meaning shifts: scarves/scarfs; fish/fishes; coffee/two coffees.
Designed for deliberate practice: meaning → category → role → form → exception.
