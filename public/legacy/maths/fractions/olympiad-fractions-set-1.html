1. Part–Whole

Skill focus: Reading a fraction as part of a whole; finding a part from the whole, and recovering the whole from a part.
Foundation
Q1. The bar below is divided into 8 equal parts. (a) What fraction of the bar is shaded? (b) What fraction is unshaded? (c) If the whole bar stands for 40 marbles, how many marbles does the shaded part represent?
Q2. In a class, 3/7 of the students are boys. There are exactly 12 boys. How many students are in the class, and how many are girls?
Q3. A ribbon is cut into equal pieces. Four of those pieces together make up 4/9 of the whole ribbon. Into how many equal pieces is the ribbon cut, and what fraction of the ribbon is one piece?
Challenge
Q4. In a jar, 2/5 of the sweets are red, 1/3 are green, and the remaining 20 are yellow. How many sweets are in the jar, and how many are red?
Q5. Priya reads 1/4 of a book on Monday and 40 more pages on Tuesday. She has now read exactly half the book. How many pages does the book have?
Q6. On a farm, 5/9 of the animals are chickens and the rest are goats. There are 24 more chickens than goats. How many animals are on the farm?
Q7. A container of fruit juice is 3/8 orange juice by volume, which is 15 litres. What is the total volume of juice, and how many litres are not orange?
Achiever
Q8. At a school, 4/7 of the pupils walk to school and the rest come by bus. Next term, 30 pupils who used to take the bus start walking instead, and now 5/7 of the pupils walk. The total number of pupils is unchanged. How many pupils attend the school?
Q9. A collection of coins is made of gold, silver and bronze. Exactly 1/2 of the coins are bronze. Of the coins that are not bronze, 2/3 are silver. There are 8 gold coins. How many coins are there altogether, and how many are silver?

2. Fraction of a Fraction

Skill focus: Interpreting “of” as multiplication and combining nested fractional parts.
Foundation
Q10. Find 2/3 of 3/4. Use the area model below to explain your answer.
Q11. A pizza has 3/4 of it left. Sam eats 1/3 of what is left. What fraction of the whole pizza did Sam eat, and what fraction of the whole pizza now remains?
Q12. Exactly half of a rope is painted. Of the painted part, 2/5 is painted red. What fraction of the whole rope is red?
Challenge
Q13. A field is 5/6 planted. Of the planted part, 3/10 is wheat. The wheat covers 25 m². What is the area of the whole field?
Q14. In a bag of counters, 2/5 are blue. Of the blue counters, 3/4 are large. There are 18 large blue counters. How many counters are in the bag?
Q15. A tank is 7/8 full. One quarter of the water in it is then used. What fraction of the tank's capacity of water remains?
Q16. On a shelf, 3/5 of the books are novels. Of the novels, 1/6 are historical. Of those historical novels, 1/2 are hardback. What fraction of all the books are hardback historical novels? If there are 4 such books, how many books are on the shelf?
Achiever
Q17. A jug is 5/6 full of squash. Maya pours out 2/5 of the squash into glasses, then drinks 1/2 of what remains in the jug. The jug now holds 300 mL of squash. What is the full capacity of the jug?
Q18. Some sweets are shared out. First 1/3 of them are given to Ann. Of the remainder, 1/4 are given to Ben. Of what is then left, 2/5 are given to Cara. After this, 27 sweets remain. How many sweets were there at the start, and how many did Ben receive?

3. Reverse Fraction (Working Backwards)

Skill focus: Undoing a chain of fractional operations to recover the starting amount.
Foundation
Q19. Three-fifths of a number is 24. What is the number?
Q20. After spending 2/7 of her money, Lily has £20 left. How much did she start with?
Q21. A number is multiplied by 3/4, and the result is 18. That result is then doubled. What is the final value, and what was the original number?
Challenge
Q22. Tom gives 1/3 of his stickers to Sam, then 1/4 of what remains to Ella. He now has 18 stickers. How many did he start with?
Q23. A tank loses 1/5 of its water, after which 3/8 of what remains evaporates. 60 litres are left. How much water was in the tank at the start?
Q24. A number is increased by 1/4 of itself, giving 45. What is the number?
Q25. Priya reads a book over three days. Day 1: 1/4 of the book. Day 2: 2/3 of the remainder. Day 3: the final 30 pages. How many pages does the book have?
Achiever
Q26. A shopkeeper sells 1/2 of his apples in the morning, then 1/3 of the remainder at noon, then 2/5 of what is then left in the afternoon. In the evening he has 36 apples. How many apples did he start with, and how many did he sell at noon?
Q27. A machine takes an input, then: subtracts 5, then multiplies by 2/3, then adds 4. The output is 20. Working backwards, find the input. Then, if that output (20) is fed in as a new input, what is the new output?

4. Comparing Fractions

Skill focus: Deciding which fraction is larger using benchmarks, complements and cross-multiplication — without converting to decimals.
Foundation
Q28. Which is larger, 3/5 or 5/8? Justify without converting to decimals.
Q29. Fill each blank with >, <, or =:  (a) 4/9 ___ 1/2   (b) 7/10 ___ 2/3   (c) 6/8 ___ 9/12.
Q30. Which fraction is closer to 1: 7/8 or 11/12? Explain using the distance of each from 1 (its complement).
Challenge
Q31. Using 1/2 as a benchmark, state whether each fraction is less than, equal to, or greater than 1/2:  5/11, 9/16, 7/14, 13/25.
Q32. Which is greater, 12/17 or 15/22? Justify with cross-multiplication.
Q33. The fractions 7/12 and 7/13 share a numerator. Without dividing, which is larger and why? Then decide which is larger, 7/12 or 8/13.
Q34. For fractions a/b and c/d with a/b < c/d, a student claims the “in-between” fraction (a+c)/(b+d) always lies strictly between them. Test the claim on 1/3 and 1/2: form (1+1)/(3+2) and check whether it lies between 1/3 and 1/2.
Achiever
Q35. Find a fraction with denominator 20 that lies strictly between 2/5 and 1/2. Then find a fraction with denominator 40 that lies strictly between 2/5 and the fraction you just found. Justify each.
Q36. Arrange from smallest to largest, justifying every comparison without decimals:  5/8, 7/11, 2/3, 9/14.

5. Fractions on the Number Line

Skill focus: Placing and reading fractions on a line, and computing gaps, midpoints and positions.
Foundation
Q37. On the number line below, the segment from 0 to 1 is split into 8 equal parts. Point P sits at the 3rd tick from 0 and point Q at the 6th tick. (a) What fractions do P and Q represent? (b) What is the distance PQ, as a fraction?
Q38. On a number line, a point sits exactly halfway between 1/4 and 3/4. What fraction is it?
Q39. What fraction is exactly one-third of the way from 0 to 1? Two-thirds of the way? And what fraction is one-third of the way from 1/2 to 1?
Challenge
Q40. On a number line, A = 2/5 and B = 4/5. M is the midpoint of AB, and N is the midpoint of AM. Find M and N.
Q41. A line from 0 to 2 is divided into 12 equal parts. In lowest terms, what value sits at the 9th tick from 0? What sits at the 8th tick?
Q42. Marks are placed at 1/6 and 5/6. The segment between them is divided into 4 equal parts. What fraction is at the first division point after 1/6?
Q43. A point X on the number line is chosen so that the distance from 0 to X is 3/4 of the distance from X to 2. Find X.
Achiever
Q44. Points P, Q, R lie on a number line in that order (left to right). It is given that PQ = 1/3, QR = 2/5, and R is at 6/5. Find P and Q, and the midpoint of PR.
Q45. A frog starts at 0. Each jump takes it exactly 1/2 of the remaining distance to the point 1. So after jump 1 it is at 1/2, after jump 2 at 3/4, and so on. (a) Where is the frog after the 4th jump? (b) What fraction of the way to 1 is still left after the 4th jump? (c) Will the frog ever land exactly on 1? Explain.

6. Remaining Fraction Problems

Skill focus: Tracking “what is left” through several stages, and telling apart a fraction of the whole from a fraction of the remainder.
Foundation
Q46. A cake is cut up. 1/4 is eaten at lunch and 1/3 at dinner. What fraction of the cake remains?
Q47. In a class, 2/5 chose football and 1/4 chose hockey; the rest chose neither. What fraction chose neither?
Q48. A jug is full. 1/3 of the full amount is poured out, then 1/4 of the original full amount is poured out. What fraction of the jug's contents remains?
Challenge
Q49. A worker paints 1/3 of a wall on day 1. On day 2 he paints 1/2 of the remaining wall. What fraction of the whole wall is still unpainted after day 2?
Q50. From a roll of tape, Ann uses 2/7. Ben then uses 1/2 of what is left. What fraction of the whole roll is left for Cara?
Q51. A tank is full. Monday: 1/4 is used. Tuesday: 1/3 of the remainder is used. Wednesday: 1/2 of the new remainder is used. What fraction of the tank is left on Wednesday evening?
Q52. Of a pile of marbles, 1/5 are lost, then 1/4 of the remainder are given away, then 1/3 of the new remainder are put in a box. What fraction of the original marbles is neither lost, given away, nor boxed?
Achiever
Q53. A basket of eggs: 1/5 break on the way home. Of the unbroken eggs, 3/8 are used for baking. Of what then remains, 3/5 are boiled. After all this, 8 eggs are left raw and unbroken. How many eggs were in the basket at the start, and how many were used for baking?
Q54. A sum of money is spent over a week. Each day, exactly 1/2 of the money present at the start of that day is spent. After 5 days, £3 remains. How much money was there at the start of day 1? Express the amount spent on day 3 as a fraction of the original sum.

7. Area with Fractional Dimensions

Skill focus: Areas of rectangles with fractional side lengths, and finding a missing side or a composite (L-shaped) area.
Foundation
Q55. A rectangle is 3/4 m long and 2/3 m wide. Find its area.
Q56. A square has side 5/6 cm. Find its area.
Q57. A rectangle is 4/5 m long and has area 2/5 m². Find its width.
Challenge
Q58. A rectangular garden is 7/2 m by 4/3 m. Find its area as a mixed number.
Q59. A rectangle has area 15/8 m² and width 5/4 m. Find its length, then its perimeter.
Q60. A large rectangle is split into a 2-by-3 arrangement of 6 equal small rectangles, each of area 5/12 m². Find the area of the large rectangle. If it is 5/3 m wide, how long is it?
Q61. A rectangular field measures 6/5 km by 5/3 km. Exactly half of it is planted with corn, and 3/5 of the corn area is ripe. What is the ripe corn area?
Achiever
Q62. A rectangle is 9/4 m long and 5/3 m wide. A smaller rectangle of length 3/2 m and width 5/6 m is cut from one corner. Find the area of the remaining L-shaped piece.
Q63. A rectangular sheet is 8/3 m long and 3/2 m wide. (a) It is cut into strips of width 1/3 m running the full length. How many whole strips fit across the width, and how much width is left over? (b) What is the area of one such strip? (c) Instead, the sheet is divided into 4 equal-area rectangles by cuts parallel to the width. What is the length of each of these?

8. Tanks and Pipes

Skill focus: Fractions of a tank's capacity under filling and draining, and combining fill/empty amounts per hour.
Foundation
Q64. A tank is 3/5 full. Then an amount equal to 1/10 of the tank's capacity is added. What fraction of the tank is now full?
Q65. A pipe fills 1/8 of a tank each hour. What fraction is filled after 3 hours? How many hours does it take to fill the whole tank?
Q66. A tank is full. A drain empties 1/6 of the capacity each hour. What fraction remains after 2 hours, and after how many hours is it empty?
Challenge
Q67. Pipe A fills 1/4 of a tank per hour and pipe B fills 1/6 per hour. Both run together. What fraction is filled after 1 hour, and how long to fill the whole tank?
Q68. A tank is 1/3 full. Pipe A adds an amount equal to 1/2 of the capacity, then a drain removes 1/4 of the capacity. What fraction is now in the tank?
Q69. A full tank drains through a pipe that removes 1/5 of the water present each hour (not 1/5 of the capacity). What fraction of the original water remains after 2 hours?
Q70. Pipe A fills 1/3 of a tank per hour; drain B empties 1/12 per hour. Starting empty with both open, after how many hours is the tank exactly 5/6 full?
Achiever
Q71. A tank is 1/2 full. Pipe A alone would fill the empty tank in 4 hours; drain B alone would empty the full tank in 6 hours. With both open from the 1/2-full start, will the tank fill or empty — and how long until it reaches 5/6 full (if filling) or 1/6 full (if emptying)?
Q72. A tank starts empty. Pipe X fills 5/12 of the tank per hour, pipe Y fills 1/4 per hour, and drain Z empties 5/6 per hour. In hour 1, only X and Y run. From hour 2 onward, all three run together. (a) What fraction is in the tank after hour 1? (b) What is the net change per hour once Z is open? (c) After how many total hours does the tank first return to empty (reach 0)?

9. Balance Scale Problems

Skill focus: Turning a balanced scale into an equation and solving for an unknown fractional mass.
Foundation
Q73. The balance below is level: one box on the left balances 3/4 kg of weights on the right. What is the mass of the box?
Q74. Two identical apples balance 1/2 kg. What is the mass of one apple?
Q75. On a balance, 3 identical blocks on the left balance 2 identical blocks plus a 1/2 kg weight on the right. Find the mass of one block.
Challenge
Q76. One melon balances 4 identical oranges, and one orange weighs 1/6 kg. What is the mass of the melon, and what fraction of the melon's mass is one orange?
Q77. A brick balances 3/4 of a brick together with a 1/2 kg weight. Find the mass of a whole brick.
Q78. Left pan: 2 identical tins. Right pan: 1 tin plus a 3/5 kg weight plus a 1/10 kg weight. Find the mass of one tin.
Q79. The left pan holds 5 equal marbles; the right pan holds 2 equal marbles and a bag weighing 9/10 kg. All marbles are identical. Find the mass of one marble.
Achiever
Q80. A large weight and a small weight together balance 2 kg. Also, the large weight on its own balances the small weight together with a 2/3 kg weight. Find the mass of each of the two weights.
Q81. Three coin types: gold, silver, bronze. One gold balances two silver; one silver balances three bronze; and one gold, one silver and one bronze together weigh 5/2 kg. Find the mass of each coin type, and state what fraction of the gold coin's mass a bronze coin is.

10. Recipe Scaling

Skill focus: Scaling fractional ingredient amounts up or down by whole and fractional factors.
Foundation
Q82. A recipe for 4 people needs 3/4 cup of flour. How much flour is needed for 8 people?
Q83. A recipe uses 2/3 cup of sugar to make 6 muffins. How much sugar for 3 muffins?
Q84. A drink needs 1/2 litre of juice per jug. How much juice for 3 jugs? For half a jug?
Challenge
Q85. A recipe for 4 servings needs 2/3 cup of milk. How much milk for 6 servings?
Q86. One batch uses 3/4 cup of oats and 2/5 cup of raisins. You make 5/2 batches. How much of each ingredient do you need?
Q87. A large cake needs 9/2 cups of flour. You want to make only 2/3 of the cake. How much flour is needed?
Q88. A batch of 8 pancakes uses 5/6 cup of batter mix. You have exactly 5/2 cups of mix. How many whole pancakes can you make, if batter is the only limit?
Achiever
Q89. A recipe for 6 servings requires 3/4 cup sugar, 2/3 cup flour and 1/2 cup butter. A baker wants 15 servings but has only 5/3 cups of sugar. (a) By what factor must the recipe be scaled for 15 servings? (b) How much sugar do 15 servings need, and is there enough? (c) With only the sugar available, what is the largest whole number of servings the baker can make?
Q90. A syrup is mixed so that sugar makes up 2/5 of the total volume. You make 3/2 litres of syrup. (a) How much sugar and how much water does it contain? (b) You then stir in an extra 1/2 litre of pure water. What fraction of the new mixture is sugar?

11. Shaded Geometry

Skill focus: Reading a fractional area straight from a partitioned figure, and adding fractional regions of different sizes.
Foundation
Q91. The rectangle is divided into 6 equal squares. What fraction is shaded, in lowest terms?
Q92. The circle is divided into 8 equal sectors. What fraction is shaded, and what fraction is unshaded?
Q93. A square is split by its two diagonals into 4 triangles. What fraction of the square is one shaded triangle (as drawn)? What fraction would two opposite triangles be?
Challenge
Q94. In the 4×4 grid, an L-shape of cells is shaded. What fraction of the grid is shaded, in lowest terms?
Q95. In the rectangle, the whole left half is shaded, and in the right half only the top half is shaded. What fraction of the whole rectangle is shaded?
Q96. A large square is split into 4 equal quarters. The top-left quarter is itself split into 4, with one small part shaded; the bottom-right quarter is fully shaded. What fraction of the large square is shaded?
Q97. A rectangle is split into 3 equal horizontal strips. The middle strip is split into 4 equal parts with 3 shaded; the bottom strip is fully shaded. What fraction of the rectangle is shaded?
Achiever
Q98. A square is divided into a 3×3 grid of 9 equal cells. The centre cell is fully shaded, and in each of the 4 corner cells exactly half (a triangle) is shaded. What fraction of the big square is shaded?
Q99. A rectangle is split in half vertically. The left half is divided into 3 equal horizontal strips (2 shaded); the right half is divided into 4 equal horizontal strips (1 shaded). (a) What fraction of the whole rectangle is shaded? (b) If the whole rectangle has area 24 cm², what is the shaded area?

12. Fraction Puzzles

Skill focus: Creative reasoning with unit fractions, hidden constraints and digit conditions.
Foundation
Q100. Write 3/4 as a sum of two different unit fractions (each of the form 1/n).
Q101. Find the missing number: 1/2 + 1/? = 5/6.
Q102. Find a fraction equal to 1/2 whose numerator and denominator add up to 15.
Challenge
Q103. Express 2/3 as a sum of two different unit fractions.
Q104. Find every pair of different unit fractions that add to 1/2 with both denominators at most 6.
Q105. A fraction equal to 3/8 has numerator and denominator adding to 33. Find the fraction.
Q106. Find all pairs of whole numbers a ≤ b with 1/a + 1/b = 1/2. Show your reasoning.
Achiever
Q107. Express 5/8 as a sum of distinct unit fractions using the greedy method: at each step subtract the largest unit fraction that does not exceed what remains. Show the steps.
Q108. A fraction has this property: adding 1 to both its numerator and denominator gives 3/4; subtracting 1 from both gives 2/3. Find the original fraction.

Full Worked Solutions

1 · Part–Whole

Q1. The bar has 8 equal parts with 5 shaded. (a) Shaded = 5/8. (b) Unshaded = 3/8. (c) 40 × 5/8 = 25 marbles.
Q2. 3/7 of the class = 12, so the whole = 12 × 7/3 = 28 students; girls = 28 − 12 = 16.
Q3. 4 pieces make 4/9, so 1 piece = 1/9 and the ribbon is cut into 9 equal pieces.
Q4. Red + green = 2/5 + 1/3 = 11/15, so yellow = 4/15 = 20, giving total = 20 × 15/4 = 75 sweets; red = 2/5 × 75 = 30.
Q5. Monday 1/4 plus 40 pages equals half, so 40 pages = 1/2 − 1/4 = 1/4 of the book ⇒ book = 160 pages.
Q6. Chickens 5/9, goats 4/9; the difference 1/9 = 24, so total = 216 animals (120 chickens, 96 goats).
Q7. 3/8 = 15 L, so total = 15 × 8/3 = 40 L; not orange = 25 L.
Q8. Walkers rise from 4/7 to 5/7, an increase of 1/7 = 30 pupils ⇒ total = 210 pupils. (Check: 120→150 walk, 90→60 bus.)
Q9. Bronze = 1/2. Of the other 1/2, silver is 2/3 (= 1/3 of all) and gold is 1/3 of that half (= 1/6 of all). So 1/6 = 8 ⇒ total = 48 coins; silver = 1/3 × 48 = 16.

2 · Fraction of a Fraction

Q10. 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2. In the model, the double-shaded region is 6 of the 12 cells.
Q11. Sam eats 1/3 × 3/4 = 1/4 of the whole; remaining = 3/4 − 1/4 = 1/2.
Q12. Red = 2/5 × 1/2 = 1/5 of the rope.
Q13. Wheat fraction of the field = 3/10 × 5/6 = 1/4. So 1/4 = 25 m² ⇒ field = 100 m².
Q14. Large blue = 3/4 × 2/5 = 3/10 of all = 18 ⇒ total = 60 counters.
Q15. A quarter is used, so 3/4 of the water remains: 3/4 × 7/8 = 21/32 of capacity.
Q16. Fraction = 1/2 × 1/6 × 3/5 = 3/60 = 1/20. Then 1/20 = 4 ⇒ 80 books.
Q17. After pouring out 2/5: 3/5 × 5/6C = 1/2C. After drinking half of that: 1/2 × 1/2C = 1/4C = 300 mL ⇒ capacity = 1200 mL.
Q18. After Ann: 2/3. After Ben (1/4 given): 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2. After Cara (2/5 given): 3/5 × 1/2 = 3/10 = 27 ⇒ start = 90 sweets. Ben got 1/4 × 2/3 × 90 = 15.

3 · Reverse Fraction (Working Backwards)

Q19. 3/5 of N = 24 ⇒ N = 24 × 5/3 = 40.
Q20. Money left = 5/7 = £20 ⇒ start = 20 × 7/5 = £28.
Q21. Original × 3/4 = 18 ⇒ original = 24. Doubling 18 gives final = 36.
Q22. After Sam: 2/3. After Ella: 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2 = 18 ⇒ start = 36 stickers.
Q23. After the loss: 4/5. After evaporation: 5/8 × 4/5 = 1/2 = 60 ⇒ start = 120 L.
Q24. x + 1/4x = 5/4x = 45 ⇒ x = 36.
Q25. After Day 1: 3/4 left. Day 2 reads 2/3 of it, leaving 1/3 × 3/4 = 1/4 = 30 pages ⇒ book = 120 pages (30, 60, 30).
Q26. After morning: 1/2. Noon leaves 2/3 × 1/2 = 1/3. Afternoon leaves 3/5 × 1/3 = 1/5 = 36 ⇒ start = 180 apples; noon sold = 1/3 × 90 = 30.
Q27. Reverse: 20 − 4 = 16; ÷ 2/3 → 24; + 5 → input = 29. Feeding 20 forward: 20−5=15, ×2/3=10, +4 = 14.

4 · Comparing Fractions

Q28. Cross-multiply: 3×8 = 24 and 5×5 = 25; since 25 > 24, 5/8 > 3/5.
Q29. (a) 8/18 < 9/18 so <. (b) 21/30 > 20/30 so >. (c) 6/8 = 3/4 = 9/12 so =.
Q30. Distances from 1: 1 − 7/8 = 1/8; 1 − 11/12 = 1/12. Since 1/12 < 1/8, 11/12 is closer to 1.
Q31. Compare numerator with half the denominator: 5/11 < 1/2 (5<5.5); 9/16 > 1/2 (9>8); 7/14 = 1/2; 13/25 > 1/2 (13>12.5).
Q32. 12×22 = 264 and 15×17 = 255; since 264 > 255, 12/17 > 15/22.
Q33. Same numerator ⇒ larger denominator gives the smaller fraction, so 7/12 > 7/13. For 7/12 vs 8/13: 7×13 = 91 < 8×12 = 96, so 8/13 > 7/12.
Q34. The in-between fraction is 2/5. Check: 1/3 vs 2/5 gives 5 < 6 so 1/3 < 2/5; 2/5 vs 1/2 gives 4 < 5 so 2/5 < 1/2. So it does lie strictly between here.
Q35. 2/5 = 8/20 and 1/2 = 10/20, so 9/20 lies between (the only choice with denominator 20). Then 2/5 = 16/40 and 9/20 = 18/40, so 17/40 lies between those.
Q36. 5/8 vs 7/11: 55 < 56 ⇒ 5/8 < 7/11. 7/11 vs 9/14: 98 < 99 ⇒ 7/11 < 9/14. 9/14 vs 2/3: 27 < 28 ⇒ 9/14 < 2/3. Order: 5/8 < 7/11 < 9/14 < 2/3.

5 · Fractions on the Number Line

Q37. Each part is 1/8, so P = 3/8 and Q = 6/8 = 3/4. Distance PQ = 3/8.
Q38. Midpoint = (1/4 + 3/4) ÷ 2 = 1/2.
Q39. 1/3 and 2/3. From 1/2 to 1 the gap is 1/2; one-third along = 1/2 + 1/6 = 2/3.
Q40. M = (2/5 + 4/5) ÷ 2 = 3/5; N = (2/5 + 3/5) ÷ 2 = 1/2.
Q41. Each part = 2/12 = 1/6. 9th tick = 9/6 = 3/2; 8th tick = 8/6 = 4/3.
Q42. Gap = 5/6 − 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3; divided by 4 gives 1/6 each. First point = 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3.
Q43. X = 3/4(2 − X) ⇒ X + 3/4X = 3/2 ⇒ 7/4X = 3/2 ⇒ X = 6/7.
Q44. R = 6/5; Q = 6/5 − 2/5 = 4/5; P = 4/5 − 1/3 = 7/15. Midpoint PR = (7/15 + 18/15) ÷ 2 = 5/6.
Q45. After n jumps the frog is at 1 − (1/2)n. (a) 1 − 1/16 = 15/16. (b) Left = 1/16. (c) No — (1/2)n is always greater than 0, so the position is always less than 1.

6 · Remaining Fraction Problems

Q46. Remaining = 1 − 1/4 − 1/3 = 1 − 7/12 = 5/12.
Q47. Neither = 1 − 2/5 − 1/4 = 1 − 13/20 = 7/20.
Q48. Both amounts are of the full jug: 1 − 1/3 − 1/4 = 5/12 remains.
Q49. After day 1: 2/3 unpainted. Day 2 paints half of that (1/3), leaving 1/2 × 2/3 = 1/3 unpainted.
Q50. After Ann: 5/7. Ben uses half, leaving 1/2 × 5/7 = 5/14.
Q51. 3/4 → 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2 → 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4 left.
Q52. 4/5 → 3/4 × 4/5 = 3/5 → 2/3 × 3/5 = 2/5 still in hand.
Q53. Unbroken = 4/5. After baking: 5/8 × 4/5 = 1/2. After boiling: 2/5 × 1/2 = 1/5 = 8 ⇒ start = 40 eggs. Baking used 3/8 × 32 = 12.
Q54. Five halvings leave (1/2)5 = 1/32 = £3 ⇒ start = £96. Day 3 spends (1/2)3 = 1/8 of the original (= £12).

7 · Area with Fractional Dimensions

Q55. Area = 3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12 = 1/2 m².
Q56. Area = (5/6)² = 25/36 cm².
Q57. Width = area ÷ length = 2/5 ÷ 4/5 = 2/5 × 5/4 = 1/2 m.
Q58. Area = 7/2 × 4/3 = 28/6 = 14/3 = 4 2/3 m².
